project_summary = """
This iGEM project explored a synthetic biology strategy for reducing the immunogenic burden of gluten in the intestine using engineered probiotic bacteria. The core concept was to help *E. coli* remain near the intestinal surface and locally deliver the gluten-degrading enzymes Fvp and PEP. To support this goal, the project combined adhesion, enzyme expression, and environmental sensing in a modular design.

A key focus was making the system responsive to intestinal conditions rather than relying on constitutive expression. We tested adhesion and enzyme-expression constructs with fluorescent reporters, explored acid-responsive regulation, and later investigated an NO-responsive sensor as a possible link to inflammatory conditions. Wet-lab work included plasmid assembly, fluorescence imaging, growth measurements, and preliminary adhesion testing.

The results were mixed but informative. Some constructs showed detectable expression or successful growth, while others required redesign. Further work is still needed to confirm efficient enzyme release after adhesion and, most importantly, whether the released enzymes can effectively degrade gluten peptides.
"""
