project_summary = """
This project investigates how botulinum neurotoxin (BoNT)-related modulation may affect ankle impedance and lower-limb control during post-stroke walking.

My role spans both the human-subject experimental workflow and the processing of Vicon motion-capture data, with a focus on quantifying lower-limb responses to controlled mechanical perturbations. I analyzed shank motion, acceleration, and joint-torque responses following perturbation and compared these features between post-stroke and control participants. These measurements provide a way to examine changes in neuromotor control that may not be apparent from conventional gait measures alone, while also supporting biomechanical models of post-stroke walking and the quantitative evaluation of BoNT-related effects.
