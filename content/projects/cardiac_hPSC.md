project_summary = """
This final-year research project investigated how small-molecule signaling could be used to better control early cardiac differentiation from human embryonic stem cells. Using a chemically defined E8-based system, I focused on a simple question: how much can cell fate be redirected by changing when and how developmental signals are introduced?

The study built on an early-passaging strategy designed to remodel cell adhesion and improve cardiac differentiation (<a href="https://doi.org/10.7150/ijbs.120853" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Liu et al., 2025</a>). Day 2 passaging produced more uniform cultures and stronger cardiac marker expression. From there, I examined how BMP signaling and retinoic acid influenced emerging lineage identity. Sequential BMP4 and LDN-193189 treatment showed potential to shift early transcriptional profiles toward a second heart field-associated state while preserving cardiomyocyte differentiation. Retinoic acid was more context-dependent, with its effects changing across dose and timing and appearing to influence subtype-associated and maturation-related markers differently.

Not every culture followed a clearly cardiac trajectory. Some cells developed morphology that appeared more myogenic than typical cardiomyocytes, but this was not confirmed at the molecular level. Overall, the project highlighted how sensitive early cardiac differentiation is to signaling timing, while also showing the importance of separating suggestive phenotypes from validated lineage identity.
"""

## Key Methods

- hESC maintenance
- 2D monolayer cardiac differentiation
- WNT pathway modulation
- qRT-PCR and western blot
- Flow cytometry
- Immunofluorescence and confocal microscopy

## Main Outputs

- Day 2 passaging improved morphology and cardiac-marker expression
- BMP4/LDN-193189 shifted cells toward SHF-associated marker profiles
- Low-dose RA effects depended on timing and dose
